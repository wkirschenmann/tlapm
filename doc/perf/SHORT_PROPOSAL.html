tlapm · performance

Nine pull requests to make large proofs tractable

Seventeen commits, one subject each, measured commit by commit on six specifications with five metrics a user can time from outside the tool. Two of those specifications cannot be prepared at all today.

branch tlapm-perf-short17 files, +882 / −181base 4600b24

Machine assistance has made it realistic to generate TLA+ proofs rather than write them by hand. What comes out is markedly more verbose than what a person writes, and much longer: specifications carrying ten or twenty thousand obligations are no longer unusual.

tlapm was proven on proofs of a different size. It is comfortable on small ones and does not scale to these: the cost of an obligation grows with the file it sits in, so the work grows faster than the proof does.

We went looking for where that growth comes from. It is localised — a handful of places where a non-linear cost sits inside a loop over the context — which is why what follows is nine pull requests against the existing design rather than a different one.

02

One mechanism, four consequences

tlapm generates one obligation per proof node, and each obligation carries its whole context: the module scope, every definition in scope, and the statement of every theorem proved before it. The context therefore grows with the file, and the per-obligation work is a function of the context. Generating all of them is therefore O(n²).

That single fact has four distinct consequences, and this series has one family of change for each:

Repeated passes over the context

Expanding k definitions walked the context k times; detecting ENABLED axioms compared every axiom against every hypothesis; every hypothesis lookup walked a deque.

PR2, PR3, PR7

Contexts kept alive

Every obligation was materialised before the first prover started, and a memo table pinned one context per obligation. Peak memory grew with the file.

PR4

Context that no longer matters

After expansion, hidden definitions and the statements of earlier theorems are unreachable from the goal, and are most of the weight shipped to the prover.

PR5

Context recomputed from scratch

Consecutive obligations share almost all of their context — on the 30k monolith, 699 of 743 hypotheses are the physically same objects as in the previous one — and all three preparation passes recomputed the whole thing.

The order is not the order the work happened in; it is the order the measurements support. Each of the first six pull requests either crosses a threshold — a specification that could not be prepared now can — or moves a metric by a ratio well clear of its spread. 05

Relation to issue #286

Six of these seventeen commits credit tlaplus/tlapm#286, and the issue itself describes four families of optimisation. The counts differ, and here is why.

The reference patchset put seven independent micro-optimisations in a single commit. Splitting that batch one subject per commit is the whole point of the exercise — each becomes reviewable on its own, and each becomes measurable on its own. Measuring them separately is what showed that five of the seven move nothing, so they are in §6 rather than here. Two survive: the deque lookups and the scheduler reaper. Add the single-pass expansion, the two prunes, and the linear ENABLED scan, and that is the six.

The other eleven commits are new. Three are the timing defects, two more are scheduler fixes, two are the memory pull request, three are the prefix-resume caches, one is the editor's obligation pool and one memoizes the grammar — and the pool is in a component the issue does not touch at all.

06

What is deliberately not here

Nine further commits were written, measured, and are not proposed. Each is correct and each removes real work; none of them moves a metric a user can observe. They are listed because the reason they are absent is a result.

The ranges are the ratios over every corpus and both of generation and preparation, from a separate larger campaign on a different machine. Their absolute timings do not transfer; the ratios do, and every one of these nine straddles 1 — a ratio that changes sign between two corpora of the same tool is measuring the run, not the change.

The bar is a sustained step, not a large single ratio, and applying it changed a decision. A tenth commit — memoizing the two instances of each grammar rule — was on this list because the editor-latency harness could not resolve it at ten repetitions. Re-reading the same campaign by corpus showed a step on generation of the two private specifications that the following nine commits all sustain. It is now PR9. The lesson is in the method rather than in the commit: one metric failing to see a change is not the same as the change not being there.

What is still open after this series is a different kind of change rather than a smaller one, which is why none of it is here: the editor re-elaborates the whole document on every interaction and then a child process repeats the work; there is no in-process cache of elaborated EXTENDS dependencies; and obligations are generated eagerly for the whole file even when the request concerns one proof. Each of those is an architectural change with a design discussion in front of it, not a commit.

Two figures here come from a different campaign and say so where they appear: §6's nine ranges, from a larger campaign on another machine, and the sharing figure in §2, from a TLAPM_PREP_SHARE probe on the private monolith.

The two private specifications are a customer's and are not published — only the measurements taken on them are.
