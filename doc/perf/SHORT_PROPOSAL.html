What breaks, and where

tlapm is fine on small proofs and unusable on large ones, and the boundary is not gradual. The five specifications below are the same tool on the same machine: seventy obligations finish before you notice, and ten thousand do not finish at all.

The failure is not slowness. On the monolith main exhausts the 12 GB address space before it finishes preparing, and on the refinement chain no run of main has finished preparing.

The same wall stands in the editor. Re-checking the refinement chain after a single edit, with every fingerprint already in the cache, has never finished on main and takes 32.9 s after this series.

And at the keystroke, on the private 30k monolith: from didChange to publishDiagnostics, 122.5 s on main against 11.3 s after — ×10.8. That is the wait for one typed character, with every fingerprint already cached.

The constraint that shapes everything below: small proofs must not get slower. Every chart carries a 71-obligation module for exactly that reason — it is the control, not a result.
02

One mechanism, four consequences

One mechanism explains all of it. tlapm generates one obligation per proof leaf, and each obligation carries its whole context: the module scope, every definition in scope, and the statement of every theorem proved before it. The context therefore grows with the file, and the per-obligation work is a function of the context. Work per obligation grows with file size, and total work grows faster than the number of obligations.

That single fact has four distinct consequences, and this series has one family of change for each:

Repeated passes over the context

Expanding k definitions walked the context k times; detecting ENABLED axioms compared every axiom against every hypothesis; every hypothesis lookup walked a deque. Each is a factor of context size that need not be there.

PR2, PR3, PR7

Contexts kept alive

Every obligation of the run was materialised before the first prover started, and a process-lifetime memo table pinned one context per obligation prepared. Peak memory grew with the file, so a big enough file cannot run at any speed.

PR4

Context that no longer matters

After expansion, the hidden definitions and the instantiated statements of earlier theorems are unreachable from the goal — and are most of the weight shipped to the prover. Dropping them sends strictly less than today.

PR5

Context recomputed from scratch

Consecutive obligations share almost all of their context — on a 30k module, 699 of 743 hypotheses are the physically same objects as in the previous one, measured on the private monolith with the TLAPM_PREP_SHARE probe — and all three preparation passes recomputed the whole thing.

PR6

The mechanism, in seven small modules and one real proof

doc/perf/short/instance_demo/ is the mechanism with nothing else in it: a three-level refinement stack shaped like the private one. Levels 0 and 1 each get the same three modules — a state module holding the parameters and the standing assumptions, a specification, and a theorems module that declares results without proving them — and each reaches the level below by EXTENDS and instantiates its theorems by INSTANCE. The largest of the seven stack modules is 92 lines. On top of them sits L2Proofs.tla, a level-2 proof of 3 239 lines that cites their instantiated theorems 178 times: 95 at one INSTANCE hop and 83 at two.

INSTANCE does not share; it copies. When level 2 instantiates level 1, level 1's body already contains a copy of level 0 — renamed once when level 1 instantiated it — so level 2 gets level 0 a second time, renamed twice. The nesting is flattened at elaboration time: by the time an obligation exists there is no indirection left to follow, only copies. The Laddern modules make that countable. Each has one obligation and differs from the next only in how many times it instantiates the same theorems module.

What that costs a proof that actually uses it

The ladder counts the copies; L2Proofs.tla shows what they cost once a proof cites them. It is 3 239 lines over 40 lemmas of two kinds, and the split matters because they stress different things. A Cite lemma (27 of them) keeps every operator opaque and chains instantiated theorems: what it exercises is the context, which carries every definition of both levels below whether or not the goal mentions it. An Open lemma (13) names definitions in DEF — 28 unfoldings in all — so the bodies of the invariants really enter the obligation, which is what a proof does when it opens an invariant to reach one conjunct and the only way a definition's weight reaches a prover at all.

Both kinds are proof trees four or five levels deep — 406 steps at depth four, 192 at depth five — which matters because every step's statement joins the context of its later siblings and of everything nested beneath it. harness/gen_l2proofs.py generates the file from the lemma index with no randomness, so regenerating it is byte-identical.

This is the corpus earning its keep. A 3 239-line proof over a stack whose own source is 264 lines takes 80 s of preparation and 1.6 GB of peak memory on the base commit, and 5.4 s and 60 MB at the tip — ×14.8 on preparation and ÷28 on memory. Every obligation is proved on both sides and the generated obligation stream is byte-identical between them (82 792 lines of --printallobs compared): the subset invariant, checked exactly on a corpus small enough for it. harness/instance_demo.sh refuses to write the CSV behind these tables if either check fails.

What it does not reproduce is scale — 2 703 obligations against the 29 965 of the private monolith — which is why two of the corpora in §4 are a customer's. This one is the part anyone can run.

Two hop-depth mistakes are easy to make in a stack like this and cost hours when they happen on a real one, because the prefix counts INSTANCE hops and not EXTENDS hops. instance_demo/CiteTrap.tla exhibits the first and is expected to report one failed obligation; instance_demo/README.md has both, and the diagnostic for either.

Two changes sit outside that mechanism. One is the editor's proof-step tree, which scanned the obligation map once per step and had nothing to do with contexts. The other is the set of correctness fixes, which are here because without them the measurements that justify the rest are not available.

04

How it was measured

Five metrics. Every one of them is something a user can time from outside tlapm with stock flags on a stock build — no probe, no patched binary, nothing this series introduces.

metrichowwhat it is
preparation timetlapm --noproving --nofpthe whole per-obligation pipeline with no prover launched: find the method, annotate constants, fingerprint, expand, normalise, test for triviality, prune, encode. Prover-independent, so it reproduces without solvers installed.

The correctness gate every commit passes

Not a benchmark gate — a soundness one. For each of the seventeen commits, in sequence: dune runtest src and dune runtest lsp green, and the test/fast suite run against the full prover stack — Z3 4.8.9, Zenon, and Isabelle 2025 with the TLA+ heap built from this repository's isabelle/ sources. The gate is fail-set identity with main, not a pass count: a newly failing test is a regression even where the count would still look healthy.

Two invariants hold across the whole series. The provers receive a subset of what they receive today, never more, and no obligation is created that does not exist today. Fingerprints do not move: the digest is computed on the const-annotated pre-expansion obligation, and every change that removes context runs after that point, on the backend path only — so --printallobs output and cache hits are unchanged by construction rather than by testing.

What can be switched, and what cannot

Three of the seventeen commits carry a switch; the other fourteen do not, and deliberately so — they are output-preserving changes to a single code path, so a flag would mean carrying two implementations of the same function and testing neither of them properly. Where a switch does exist it restores the original code rather than disabling a feature, which is what makes it usable as a differential reference.

Every measurement here is taken with the features on and every switch unset.

One pair calibrates the noise better than the baseline repeat does. The editor obligation pool changes only files under lsp/, so the command-line preparation path across it cannot differ — whatever the campaign measures there is the run-to-run spread, on a pair whose true answer is known to be zero. It measures: small 1.4 % (memory 0.11 %), 600 1.8 % (memory 0.26 %), 1 800 0.2 % (memory 0.48 %), stack 0.0 % (memory 0.05 %), chain 0.4 % (memory 3.50 %), monolith 0.3 % (memory 0.00 %). That is the floor any ratio in §6 has to clear, and it is why a commit is only credited with an effect when it is a sustained step rather than a single large ratio. Read it per corpus: the floor is widest where the run is shortest, which is one reason the ratios in §6 are quoted on the 1 800-obligation corpus and the two private specifications rather than on the small ones.

What the campaign does not contain

Preparation and peak memory are measured at every one of the 74 (commit, corpus) pairs the design calls for: all 17 commits and main on the three public corpora, and the nine pull-request endpoints on the two private ones. There are no missing cells, so nothing in §5 or §6 rests on an absent measurement.

The two private corpora are measured at the pull-request endpoints rather than at every commit. That is a deliberate bound, not an omission: a single preparation pass on the monolith is minutes when it completes and a quarter of an hour when it does not, and the question those corpora answer — does this pull request make the specification runnable — is a property of the pull request. Where a commit inside a pull request needed separating from its neighbour, the public 1 800-obligation corpus is measured at every commit and answers it.

Runs that do not finish

A run gets an hour, and the machine caps address space at 12 GB. A cross is the cap refusing an allocation: settled, and more time cannot change it. A ring is an hour spent without finishing — not a duration, only the statement that an edit-and-wait loop is not practical there.

Lines whose left end is expensive are walked from the tip towards the base, so the budget goes to the points that still complete. A point to the left of an hour-long stop is marked from it rather than run: it is the same tool with one optimisation removed and cannot be faster. A marked point is drawn as the ring it was marked from, is named as marked in the caption below the chart, and is never a figure.

05

Commit by commit

One point per commit, main at the left, in the order the series is proposed in. A red mark instead of a point means the run did not complete, and its shape says whether that is a result. A cross is a result: the run was refused memory, or it was given a full hour and still did not finish. A ring is a protocol timeout — inconclusive: this protocol’s clock stopped the run, not anything in the commit, so it says where we stopped looking and nothing about where the commit ends up. It is not a slower version of a cross; it is the absence of an answer. The tables in §6 say which of the two ways a real failure failed, because the difference matters — a change that speeds preparation up reaches the memory wall sooner, turning a run we stopped into a run the cap refused, without being a regression. Public and private corpora share each chart: hue separates them, dash separates sizes.

Commit labels are coloured by provenance: violet is a commit whose message credits tlaplus/tlapm#286, green is new here. Bold labels are the last commit of a pull request — the point a reviewer merging that pull request would land on.

The first chart is throughput rather than speedup for one reason: on the two private specifications main has no value to form a ratio against.

Relation to issue #286

Six of these seventeen commits credit tlaplus/tlapm#286, and the issue itself describes four families of optimisation. The counts differ, and here is why.

The reference patchset put seven independent micro-optimisations in a single commit. Splitting that batch one subject per commit is the whole point of the exercise — each becomes reviewable on its own, and each becomes measurable on its own. Measuring them separately is what showed that five of the seven move nothing, so they are in §8 rather than here. Two survive: the deque lookups and the scheduler reaper. Add the single-pass expansion, the two prunes, and the linear ENABLED scan, and that is the six.

The other eleven commits are new. Three are the timing defects, two more are scheduler fixes, two are the memory pull request, three are the prefix-resume caches, one is the editor's obligation pool and one memoizes the grammar — and the pool is in a component the issue does not touch at all.

What is deliberately not here

Nine further commits were written, measured, and are not proposed. Each is correct and each removes real work; none of them moves a metric a user can observe. They are listed because the reason they are absent is a result.

The ranges are the ratios over every corpus and both of generation and preparation, from a separate larger campaign on a different machine. Their absolute timings do not transfer; the ratios do, and every one of these nine straddles 1 — a ratio that changes sign between two corpora of the same tool is measuring the run, not the change.

The bar is a sustained step, not a large single ratio, and applying it changed a decision. A tenth commit — memoizing the two instances of each grammar rule — was on this list because the editor-latency harness could not resolve it at ten repetitions. Re-reading the same campaign by corpus showed a step on generation of the two private specifications that the following nine commits all sustain. It is now PR9. The lesson is in the method rather than in the commit: one metric failing to see a change is not the same as the change not being there.

What is still open after this series is a different kind of change rather than a smaller one, which is why none of it is here: the editor re-elaborates the whole document on every interaction and then a child process repeats the work; there is no in-process cache of elaborated EXTENDS dependencies; and obligations are generated eagerly for the whole file even when the request concerns one proof. Each of those is an architectural change with a design discussion in front of it, not a commit.

Every chart, table and ratio here is generated by doc/perf/short/mkshort.py from the CSVs beside it. Two figures are not, and say so where they appear: §8's nine ranges, from a larger campaign on another machine, and the sharing figure in §2, from a TLAPM_PREP_SHARE probe on the private monolith.

The two private specifications are a customer's and are not published — only the measurements taken on them are.
